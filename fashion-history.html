Front Page · Fashion History

A short history of modern fashion

How the clothes we wear today took shape, from the loose silhouettes of the 1920s to streetwear, athleisure and the return of vintage.

1920s

The modern woman

After the First World War, hemlines rose, corsets disappeared and clothes became looser and easier to move in. Jersey and knitwear moved from sportswear into everyday dressing, laying the foundations of modern casual style.

Key pieces

Drop-waist dresses · Cloche hats · Knitwear as fashion · Shorter hemlines

1950s

Casual goes mainstream

Post-war optimism brought full skirts and fitted waists for formal dress, while young people adopted jeans, white T-shirts and leather jackets, workwear and military surplus turned into a lasting casual uniform.

Key pieces

Full-skirted dresses · White T-shirts and jeans · Loafers · Leather jackets

1970s–80s

Sportswear and self-expression

Flares, natural fabrics and expressive prints defined the seventies, while the eighties brought power shoulders and the rise of sportswear as streetwear. Tracksuits and trainers began their journey from the gym to the street.

Key pieces

Flared jeans · Tracksuits · Power shoulders · Statement trainers

1990s–today

Minimalism, streetwear and comfort

The nineties favoured clean minimalism and relaxed denim; the twenty-first century blended streetwear, athleisure and vintage. Today comfort, versatility and personal style matter more than any single trend.

Key pieces

Slip dresses · Hoodies · Athleisure · Vintage and second-hand

Dates are widely cited milestones; fashion history is complex and many changes happened gradually across different places.
