The language of style, explained

Fashion Journal is an independent reference for anyone curious about clothes: what fashion words mean, where today’s styles came from and how to find a look that’s truly yours.

Every outfit tells a story, and fashion has its own vocabulary for telling it. A bias cut, an empire waist, a notch lapel: once you know the words, shopping, styling and even repairing clothes becomes easier. This journal gathers that knowledge in one place, alongside the history behind familiar pieces and three simple tools to help you put it into practice.

    A-line

    Notes from the style desk

    Practical principles

    Start with fit. No detail or label can rescue something that doesn’t fit. Shoulders, sleeve length and the waist are worth checking first; hems and waists are easy for a tailor to adjust.

    Choose a palette. Most well-dressed people rely on a few colours that work together. Neutrals like navy, grey, camel and black make a backbone; one or two favourite colours bring personality.

    Balance proportions. Pair a loose shape with a fitted one. Wide trousers look sharp with a neat top; an oversized sweater works with slim jeans or a straight skirt.

    Mind the fabric. Natural fibres such as cotton, linen and wool breathe and age well. Look at how a fabric drapes and whether it recovers when gently stretched.

    Edit accessories. One focal point, whether a bold bag, a scarf or striking earrings, usually looks more confident than several competing ones.

    Wear what you own. Before buying, try new combinations of clothes already in your wardrobe. A fresh pairing often feels as good as something new.

    Readers’ questions

    Answered by the style desk
    Is this a fashion shop?

    No. Fashion Journal is an independent reference. We explain fashion terms, history and styling ideas, but we don’t sell clothes or promote particular brands.

    How do I find my personal style?

    Notice what you reach for most, what makes you feel confident and which shapes and colours repeat in your favourite outfits. Our style quiz can help you put words to it.

    What’s the difference between couture and ready-to-wear?

    Couture is made to measure by hand in tiny numbers. Ready-to-wear, or prêt-à-porter, is produced in standard sizes for wider sale.

    Do I need to follow trends?

    Not at all. Trends can be fun to borrow from, but a wardrobe built around your own style and lifestyle will serve you better for longer.

    What is a silhouette in fashion?

    It’s the overall outline a garment creates on the body, such as A-line, fitted, oversized or straight. Choosing silhouettes you like is the key to outfits that feel balanced.

    Why does fashion history matter?

    Most of what we wear today, from trench coats to jeans, has a story. Knowing it helps you understand why clothes look the way they do and how to wear them well.
