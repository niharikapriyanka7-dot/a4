The fashion glossary

40 fashion terms explained in plain English, from A-line to yoke.

ABCDEGHJLMNPRSTWY

A

A-line
A skirt or dress that fits at the waist and gradually widens towards the hem, like a capital A.
Appliqué
Fabric shapes stitched onto a garment as decoration.
Athleisure
Sportswear-inspired clothing designed to be worn outside the gym.

B

Bias cut
Cutting fabric diagonally across the grain so it drapes and clings softly to the body.
Bodice
The part of a dress or top that covers the torso, from shoulders to waist.
Boué
A textured yarn or fabric with small loops, often used in jackets and knitwear.
Breton stripe
Horizontal navy and white stripes inspired by French sailors’ tops.

C

Capsule wardrobe
A small collection of versatile clothes that mix and match easily.
Cap sleeve
A very short sleeve that just covers the top of the shoulder.
Chambray
A lightweight plain-weave cotton with a coloured warp and white weft, similar in look to denim.
Couture
Made-to-measure, hand-finished clothing produced in very small numbers.

D

Darts
Folded and stitched wedges of fabric that shape a garment to the body.
Drape
How a fabric hangs and falls under its own weight.
Drop shoulder
A shoulder seam that sits below the natural shoulder for a relaxed fit.

E

Empire waist
A waistline that sits just under the bust, flowing loosely below.
Epaulette
A decorative shoulder strap, borrowed from military uniforms.

G

Gingham
A checked cotton fabric with even squares, usually in white and one colour.
Godet
A triangular panel inserted into a skirt to add flare.

H

Herringbone
A V-shaped zigzag weave pattern common in wool coats and jackets.
Houndstooth
A broken check pattern of jagged, four-pointed shapes.

J

Jersey
A stretchy single-knit fabric used for T-shirts and dresses.

L

Lapel
The folded flap below a jacket or coat collar.
Lookbook
A collection of photographs showing outfits or a designer’s collection.

M

Midi
A hemline that falls between the knee and the ankle.
Monochrome
An outfit in different shades of one colour.
Muslin
A plain-woven cotton, also the name for a test version of a garment.

N

Neckline
The edge of a garment around the neck, such as crew, V, scoop or square.
Notch lapel
The most common lapel style, with a V-shaped notch where collar meets lapel.

P

Peplum
A short flared strip of fabric attached at the waist of a top or jacket.
Pleat
A fold of fabric pressed or stitched in place.
Prêt-à-porter
Ready-to-wear clothing made in standard sizes.

R

Raglan sleeve
A sleeve that extends in one piece up to the collar, with a diagonal seam.
Ruching
Gathered fabric that creates a rippled, textured effect.

S

Selvedge
The self-finished edge of a woven fabric; prized on some denim.
Shift dress
A simple, straight dress that hangs from the shoulders with little shaping.
Silhouette
The overall outline or shape of a garment or outfit.

T

Tailoring
Constructing structured garments like suits and coats to fit precisely.
Tweed
A rough, durable woollen fabric, often woven in mixed colours.

W

Wrap dress
A dress that closes by wrapping one side over the other and tying at the waist.

Y

Yoke
A fitted panel across the shoulders or hips from which the rest of a garment hangs.
